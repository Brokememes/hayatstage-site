Built For Delhi's Shows

From trade fairs to government & policy expos.

Delhi is the address every national and international exhibitor eventually has to answer for. As India's capital, and the seat of central ministries, industry associations and the country's largest public-private trade bodies, its shows draw an exhibitor mix that looks different from anywhere else we build — country pavilions standing next to PSU stalls, embassy-backed delegations sharing a hall with private enterprise, and a buyer base that spans government procurement teams as much as ordinary B2B decision-makers. Hayat Stage builds for that entire mix: stalls and pavilions sized and engineered for the scale Pragati Maidan and Bharat Mandapam actually operate at, and fabricated to the protocol and structural sign-off process that comes with hosting shows at this level.

India's National & International Show Capital

No other Indian city hosts as many nationally significant and internationally attended trade fairs in one place. Delhi's position as the capital makes it the default location for shows with a government, ministry or PSU dimension — sector expos co-organised with a ministry, industry-association flagship events, and international trade fairs that bring in country pavilions and foreign trade delegations alongside private exhibitors. That mix shapes what a stall at a Delhi show actually has to do: work as a straightforward B2B lead-generation booth for one exhibitor, while sitting a few aisles from a formally protocoled country pavilion for another. We build across that entire range, not just one end of it.

What "Ready For Scale And Protocol" Actually Means

Pragati Maidan, run today largely through the Bharat Mandapam / IICC complex, is India's largest convention and exhibition complex, and it's built and operated to match that scale — which means a stricter, more formal process than a typical regional hall. Larger and pavilion-format builds go through structural review before fabrication is signed off, access and vehicle movement for load-in and load-out is scheduled and controlled rather than open, and shows with delegation visits or ceremonial elements carry additional security and movement protocol that affects where and when a build crew can work. None of this is unusual for a venue operating at IICC's scale — it's simply a different rhythm than a regional exhibition hall, and it's one we plan for from the first concept rather than discovering mid-build.

Why Exhibitors Come To Delhi From Everywhere

Delhi shows draw exhibitors well beyond the National Capital Region. National industry associations headquarter here, so their flagship annual events become the one show many companies' India leadership actually attends in person. International exhibitors and country delegations use Delhi's international trade fairs as their India entry point. And because many pan-India company headquarters and government-liaison offices sit in or near the capital, the exhibitor list at a major Delhi show tends to include head-office decision-makers who might not travel to a regional edition of the same event elsewhere. That reach is part of why this hub sees more search volume — and more serious exhibitor intent — than any single-vertical page we build for.

Planning Ahead

Planning Your Stall In Delhi.

IICC's scale brings a more formal structural and protocol process than most venues we build for. The earlier we know your hall, format and any protocol requirements, the more runway we have to get through sign-off without compressing the build schedule.

01

Confirm Hall & Format

Your Pragati Maidan / Bharat Mandapam hall, stall dimensions and format ambition — modular, custom, double-decker or full pavilion — shape how early structural review needs to start.

02

Share Brand & Content Requirements

Logo files, brand guidelines and what needs to be displayed or demoed, so signage, structure and any equipment or product staging get sized correctly from the first concept.

03

Flag Protocol & Delegation Needs

Government-adjacent exhibitors, country-pavilion participants, or shows expecting delegation visits should flag it early — it affects the structural approval path and on-site security movement.

04

Confirm Load-In & Timeline

IICC schedules access and load-in rather than leaving it open, so we build its documentation and sign-off timeline into your build schedule from day one, not as a late add-on.

FAQ

Delhi exhibition stalls, answered.

How much does an exhibition stall cost in Delhi?

It depends on stall size, format and finish level — IICC's custom and pavilion-format builds sit at a different scale than a modular stand. Share your hall size and format and we'll send an itemised Delhi quote.

Which Delhi venues do you build for?

Any Delhi venue, most commonly Pragati Maidan / Bharat Mandapam (IICC) — the city's primary complex for large-scale national and international shows.

Can you build large country or association pavilions?

Yes — multi-exhibitor pavilion structures for country delegations and industry associations are one of our specialities for Delhi's larger international trade fairs.

What does IICC's protocol requirement actually involve?

It covers structural sign-off for larger and pavilion-format builds, scheduled access for load-in and load-out, and additional security and movement protocol for shows with delegation visits or ceremonial elements — we build all of it into the fabrication schedule from the first concept.

How far in advance should we start planning a Delhi stall?

As soon as your hall and stall allocation at Pragati Maidan / Bharat Mandapam is confirmed — custom and pavilion-format builds need more structural-approval lead time than a modular stand elsewhere.

Which industries do you build for in Delhi?

All five of our core sectors show up at Delhi's national and international fairs — technology & SaaS, healthcare & medtech, energy & industrial manufacturing, consumer/retail & travel, and corporate events & conferences — each with its own dedicated Delhi page.

Do you handle dismantling and hall handover too?

Yes — breakdown and hall handover, including IICC's post-show clearance requirements, are part of every project scope.
