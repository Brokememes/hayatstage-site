Built For Technology & SaaS

Exhibition stalls engineered for Technology & SaaS exhibitors.

Technology and software exhibitors live or die on the demo. Hayat Stage builds booths designed around working screens, live product walkthroughs and clean, brand-accurate signage — plus the stages behind partner kickoffs, developer conferences and operations meetings.

What the demo actually needs

A live product demo on a Mumbai show floor lives or dies on the same basics wherever it's built — planned power runs instead of an extension lead trailing across the aisle, a demo station positioned so a buyer can actually see the screen through the crowd, and signage that carries your brand system accurately from the fascia down to the counter graphics. We plan all three into the concept stage, whether the stall is a compact NESCO booth or a premium build inside Jio World Convention Centre at BKC, where finish standards run higher than a standard trade-show hall.

Who this vertical covers

Mumbai's tech exhibitor base spans SaaS platforms running a live product tour, hardware and electronics brands with physical units on display, security and brand-protection technology, and equipment brands that need a demo bay built to hold real instrumentation rather than a laptop stand. We've built for a global electronics distributor's stall centred on a lit column display and for a brand-protection technology company showing product authentication displays on the floor — different products, same underlying need for a stall that lets the product do the talking.

Booths — and the stages behind them

A meaningful share of our Mumbai tech-vertical work isn't a public exhibition stall at all — it's the stage behind a partner kickoff, a regional sales meeting or a product launch, often run out of a BKC hotel or the Jio World Convention Centre rather than a trade-show hall. Mumbai's concentration of corporate and financial-services headquarters means a lot of technology and SaaS companies here run these events for partners and enterprise buyers rather than a public trade-show audience, and the production requirements — LED backdrops, branded stage sets, AV — sit closer to corporate event production than stall fabrication.

NESCO in Goregaon runs on trade-show-floor logistics: large-format and double-decker stands, hall access windows and dock-and-load scheduling that a Mumbai-based crew plans around in advance. Jio World Convention Centre at BKC is a different build entirely — premium finish expectations, tighter service-lift and load-in constraints, and a corporate rather than trade-show audience. We scope the two differently from the first call.

Planning Ahead

Planning Your Tech Stall In Mumbai.

Mumbai tech exhibitors are usually planning around one of two very different venues — a trade-show hall or a premium corporate convention space — and the prep work differs depending on which one you're building for.

Lead Time

A large-format NESCO stand needs fabrication lead time similar to any major trade-show build; a premium BKC corporate event can sometimes move faster but needs earlier confirmation of AV and demo requirements, since finish expectations are higher and there's less room to improvise on-site.

What To Bring To The First Call

Your brand guidelines, a list of what's being demoed and its power/space needs, and — for a BKC or hotel-based event — the venue's service-lift and load-in restrictions if you already have them. For NESCO, your hall size and stall position speed things up.

Mumbai Logistics

NESCO Goregaon and BKC sit on opposite ends of the city with very different traffic patterns at peak hours. We plan crew call times and material transport separately for each, so a Goregaon load-in schedule never gets applied to a BKC job by default.
