Built For Chennai's Shows

From auto expos to textile trade fairs.

Chennai's exhibition floor runs on real, tangible product. Tamil Nadu's status as one of India's largest auto and auto-components manufacturing clusters — often called the "Detroit of India" — means a meaningful share of Chennai's exhibitors are showing precision-machined parts, hydraulic assemblies, tooling and finished vehicles alongside the software and services stands you'd find in any city. Hayat Stage builds for both ends of that floor: stalls engineered to carry genuine hardware, and stalls built for brands whose product is a story rather than a physical object.

What "durable, on-brand" means on Chennai's floor

For a manufacturing or auto-components exhibitor, durability isn't a finishing touch — it's structural. A machined component on a plinth, a hydraulic assembly on display or a tooling jig set up for a demo each puts real weight through a small footprint, which is a different engineering problem than spreading a brochure rack across a counter. We size flooring and support structure to what's actually going on the stand, not to a generic exhibitor average, so the stall reads as engineering-credible rather than decorative — which matters on a floor where buyers are used to judging build quality the same way they'd judge a factory line.

Textiles and consumer-facing exhibitors need almost the opposite approach: less about load capacity, more about how the product is presented and how the stand photographs. Product walls, hanging displays and merchandising fixtures get more attention than structural engineering, and finishes lean toward what holds up to continuous handling — visitors touching fabric, browsing racks — across a multi-day run without looking worn by the final day.

Chennai's IT and services sector adds a third register — clean, modern stalls built around screens and brand-system accuracy rather than physical product at all, closer to what a technology exhibitor would need in any city. Knowing which register a stall needs to speak in, and often blending two of them on the same stand, is the real design problem on Chennai's floor.

Chennai Trade Centre: one venue, one rulebook

Chennai's exhibition circuit runs through a single primary venue — Chennai Trade Centre (CTC) in Nandambakkam — rather than the multi-venue rotation you'd plan around in a market like Mumbai or Delhi. That's genuinely useful for planning: CTC's structural, electrical and layout compliance requirements are the one rulebook every custom, modular or double-decker stall gets fabricated against, so once we know your hall and stall number, the compliance path is settled early rather than being re-litigated for a different building next time. Fewer venue variables means fewer surprises at load-in — stall drawings and structural sign-off follow the same process show after show.

That consistency doesn't mean CTC's requirements are light. Custom and double-decker structures still need to clear the venue's compliance process before fabrication starts, and any stand carrying real hardware — a demo bay, a machinery display, a heavier double-decker upper level — needs its floor loading and electrical plan checked against CTC's rules specifically. We treat that as a scheduled step in every Chennai build, not an afterthought bolted on before move-in week.

Wherever your show is held in Chennai.

Chennai Trade Centre (CTC), Nandambakkam

Custom, modular and double-decker stalls fabricated to CTC's compliance and layout requirements.

Chennai's exhibition circuit runs almost entirely through this one building, which is actually an advantage when it comes to planning: unlike a multi-venue city where compliance rules effectively reset for every hall, CTC's structural, electrical and layout requirements are the single rulebook every custom, modular or double-decker stall gets fabricated against here. Once your hall and stall number are confirmed, the compliance path itself isn't a moving target — it's the same process we've run before, for a different exhibitor, in the same building.

In practice, that process means stall drawings and structural sign-off for anything beyond a standard modular unit — floor loading if the stand is carrying real hardware or machinery, electrical layout for demo power, and clearances around any running equipment. We build that approval step into the schedule for every Chennai project rather than treating it as a late add-on before move-in week.

Planning Ahead

Planning your stall in Chennai.

CTC runs its own compliance and layout sign-off process for every custom or double-decker stall, so the projects that go smoothest are the ones where we know the venue rules and your footprint early — bring us in as soon as your hall position at Chennai Trade Centre is confirmed.

01

Confirm Your CTC Footprint

Hall, stall dimensions and position within Chennai Trade Centre — this decides whether modular, custom or double-decker fits, and how much compliance runway you need.

02

Tell Us What's On The Stand

Physical product, demo hardware or machinery and its rough weight and power needs, so flooring, electrical and layout get sized correctly from the concept stage.

03

Send Brand Assets

Logo files, brand colours and any existing show photography so the stand photographs consistently with your other marketing.

04

Flag Double-Decker Or Heavy-Load Plans Early

Structural drawings for double-decker builds or stands carrying real hardware need to clear CTC's compliance process before fabrication starts — the earlier we know, the less it costs you in schedule.

A modular stand for CTC typically moves faster through design and fabrication than a custom or double-decker build, because a custom structure needs its drawings and floor-loading plan cleared through the venue's own compliance process before fabrication starts. That's less of an issue for a straightforward modular unit, which is one reason it's a common choice for a first Chennai show or for exhibitors testing a smaller footprint before scaling up to a larger custom presence.

FAQ

Chennai exhibition stalls, answered.

How much does an exhibition stall cost in Chennai?

Pricing depends on stall size, format, finish level and whether the stand needs to carry heavier hardware or machinery. Share your CTC hall size and industry and Hayat Stage will send an itemised Chennai quote.

Which Chennai venues do you build for?

Chennai Trade Centre (CTC) in Nandambakkam is the city's primary exhibition venue and where the large majority of our Chennai projects are built, fabricated to CTC's compliance and layout requirements. We can build for other Chennai venues on request.

What stall formats can CTC accommodate?

Custom, modular and double-decker formats are all built regularly at CTC — the right one depends on your footprint, budget and whether you need an upper deck for hosting or meetings.

Do you build stalls for auto and manufacturing exhibitors?

Yes — Chennai's auto-components and industrial-manufacturing cluster is one of our most frequent Chennai project categories, and we engineer flooring and structure to actually carry precision components, tooling and hydraulic assemblies.

Do you also build for textiles, IT and other industries in Chennai?

Yes — alongside auto and manufacturing, we regularly build display-heavy stalls for textile and consumer exhibitors and clean, brand-accurate stands for Chennai's IT and services sector.

How far in advance should we start planning a Chennai stall?

As soon as your CTC hall allocation is confirmed. Custom and double-decker builds need more design and compliance runway than a modular stand, so earlier is better for anything beyond a standard modular footprint.

What does CTC's compliance and layout process actually involve?

CTC requires stall drawings and structural sign-off for custom and double-decker builds before fabrication starts, covering floor loading, electrical layout and clearances. We fold that approval step into the build schedule for every Chennai project.

Do you handle dismantling too?

Yes — breakdown and hall handover are part of every project scope.
